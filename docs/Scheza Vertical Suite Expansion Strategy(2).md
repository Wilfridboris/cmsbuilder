# Scheza Vertical Suite Expansion Strategy

## Executive recommendation

Scheza can expand beyond trades, but it should not become a generic “software for every business.” Its durable category is **the generative operating system for organizations whose work still runs through spreadsheets, messages, forms, and human memory**. The product should retain one platform and one brand while packaging industry-specific editions around repeatable operational workflows.

The strongest near-term expansion is not “university” or “corporate” as a whole. Those labels are too broad. The best sequence is:

1. **Scheza Service** — contractors, repair companies, cleaners, installers, and other appointment/job-based local services.
2. **Scheza Property** — small property managers, maintenance coordinators, and multi-site facility operators.
3. **Scheza Professional** — agencies, consultancies, bookkeeping firms, recruiting firms, and other client-service teams.
4. **Scheza Community** — nonprofits, community programs, associations, and grant-funded service organizations.
5. **Scheza Care** — childcare and non-clinical care operations, after compliance capabilities are strengthened.
6. **Scheza Retail Ops** and **Scheza Restaurant Ops** — operational layers that connect to existing POS products rather than replacing them.
7. **Scheza Campus Ops** and **Scheza Workplace** — departmental workflow products for universities and corporations, pursued later through pilots and partners.

This direction aligns with Scheza’s existing product principles: AI builds the system, learns the organization’s workflows, suggests improvements, and eventually performs approved routine work. The current product already supports generated relational structures, public intake forms, mobile entry, shared workspaces, imports, invoicing, workflow learning, and a future governed operator; these are reusable horizontal capabilities, not trade-only assets.[^1]

## The category Scheza owns

### Proposed category

> **Generative Operations Platform**

A Generative Operations Platform turns a plain-language description of an organization into a working operational system: structured records, relationships, forms, dashboards, workflows, reports, and eventually a supervised digital operations crew.

This definition differentiates Scheza from:

- **Generic work-management software**, which gives customers boards, tables, and templates to configure.
- **No-code databases**, which require the customer to understand data modelling and design the system.
- **Vertical systems of record**, which offer deep but rigid industry functionality.
- **AI app builders**, which can generate an application but do not necessarily learn and operate the business over time.

Scheza’s strategic boundary should be expressed as follows:

> **Scheza serves operationally complex organizations that need custom structure but cannot justify a custom-software project.**

### Expanded vision statement

> **Every small and midsize organization in Canada runs its daily operations through a system Scheza built, improves, and helps operate for them.**

A more concrete Ontario-first version is:

> **Small and midsize organizations across Ontario run their jobs, requests, clients, assets, approvals, and follow-ups through operating systems Scheza creates and improves for them.**

Both statements are written in the present tense, name the customer, describe the service, and define geographic reach. The Ontario version is the better operating vision for the next three years; the Canada version is the category vision.

### Platform promise

> **Describe how your organization works. Scheza builds the system to run it—in under a minute.**

The promise should remain stable across vertical editions. Each edition then adds a specific operational result:

- Scheza Service: **Turn completed work into organized records and paid invoices.**
- Scheza Property: **Keep every tenant request, unit, inspection, and repair accountable.**
- Scheza Professional: **Keep every client, deliverable, hour, approval, and invoice connected.**
- Scheza Community: **Track every participant, program, service, outcome, and funder report.**
- Scheza Care: **Keep enrolment, attendance, staff tasks, incidents, and family communication organized.**
- Scheza Retail Ops: **Know what is selling, running low, waiting on a supplier, or losing margin.**
- Scheza Restaurant Ops: **Control invoices, food costs, tasks, incidents, and supplier follow-ups without another spreadsheet.**
- Scheza Campus Ops: **Turn departmental forms, approvals, cases, and requests into visible workflows.**
- Scheza Workplace: **Replace scattered forms, spreadsheets, and approval chains with one generated operations workspace.**

## Expansion criteria

Scheza should enter a vertical only when its operating pattern meets most of these conditions:

- The organization manages recurring records with relationships: customers, requests, projects, locations, inventory, cases, assets, vendors, invoices, or outcomes.
- Work enters through calls, email, forms, spreadsheets, or messaging and must be assigned to someone.
- The owner or administrator lacks the time or technical skill to configure a horizontal platform.
- Existing software is either a blank canvas, fragmented across several apps, or expensive to customize.
- The workflow becomes more valuable as historical data accumulates.
- A future agent can safely observe, recommend, and eventually execute repeatable actions.
- The first useful version does not require Scheza to replace a regulated core system, payment processor, POS, student information system, electronic health record, or general ledger.

The final point is critical. Scheza should initially become the **flexible operating layer around systems of record**, not attempt to replace every core industry platform.

### Why this order

The recommended order protects focus. Professional services, property operations, and nonprofits can reuse the current relational engine, intake forms, imports, approvals, dashboards, and activity history with limited foundational change. Retail and restaurants look attractive, but meaningful products in those categories require reliable POS, payments, inventory, and accounting integrations. Universities and corporate departments fit Scheza’s workflow model, but require stronger permissions, procurement readiness, integration controls, service commitments, and security governance.

## Scheza Service

### Target segments

- HVAC, plumbing, electrical, appliance repair, locksmiths, pest control, and handyman businesses.
- Cleaning, restoration, mobile detailing, inspection, installation, and maintenance companies.
- Small equipment repair, IT field support, and commercial service contractors.
- Later extensions: construction, renovation, snow removal, landscaping contracts, and auto repair after specialized invoicing and billing rules are supported.

### Core workflow

Lead or request → quote → schedule → assign → complete work → parts/time capture → invoice → payment status → follow-up.

### Why it remains the beachhead

This is Scheza’s most validated and technically complete workflow. The existing PRD already targets short-cycle services and includes mobile field entry, public intake, relational customer/job/invoice records, compliant invoice handling, data import, and future follow-up automation. It offers the shortest path to product-market learning and revenue.[^1]

### Expansion opportunity

The edition should broaden from “contractor software” to **service operations** without immediately taking on construction accounting. That opens cleaners, installers, repair technicians, inspection businesses, security services, mobile services, and B2B maintenance providers while preserving the same operational model.

## Scheza Property

### Best initial segment

Small property-management companies, condominium service teams, student-housing operators, commercial landlords, and facilities coordinators managing approximately 20–300 units or multiple sites.

Canada’s real-estate and rental sector had 23,763 employer establishments and 464,773 non-employer or indeterminate establishments in Ontario in 2025; 77% of employer establishments nationally were micro-businesses. That total includes many entities outside Scheza’s ideal buyer, so it should be treated as a broad market signal rather than an addressable-customer count.[^2]

### Core data model

- Properties and buildings.
- Units and spaces.
- Owners, tenants, occupants, and vendors.
- Leases and renewals.
- Maintenance requests and work orders.
- Inspections, incidents, documents, and compliance dates.
- Expenses, chargebacks, rent exceptions, and owner reports.

### Strong differentiation

Established property platforms package accounting, leasing, portals, maintenance, and payments. Buildium starts at US$62 per month, with Growth at US$192 and Premium at US$400; advanced automation, API access, and some lower transaction costs sit in higher tiers. DoorLoop starts around US$69 per month and moves to US$149 and US$209 for more advanced capabilities.[^3][^4][^5]

Scheza should not begin by replacing property accounting or rent collection. Its wedge should be:

> **Tell Scheza what you manage. It builds your properties, units, tenants, vendors, inspections, and maintenance workflow—then gives every request an owner and a next step.**

The product can coexist with QuickBooks and rent-payment tools while winning on setup speed, flexible workflows, public maintenance intake, vendor assignment, inspection history, bilingual forms, and AI-generated owner reporting.

### Recommended MVP

- Import properties, units, tenants, and vendors.
- Public maintenance request form with QR code per property.
- Work-order triage and assignment.
- Photos, documents, inspection checklists, and status history.
- Vendor follow-up reminders.
- Recurring inspection and renewal alerts.
- Owner-facing monthly operations snapshot.

Avoid rent accounting, trust accounting, tenant screening, and legal notice automation in the first release.

## Scheza Professional

### Target segments

- Marketing and creative agencies.
- IT consultancies and managed-service providers.
- Bookkeeping and fractional operations firms.
- Recruiting and staffing boutiques.
- Engineering and design consultancies.
- Translation, research, training, and grant-writing firms.

Ontario had 70,365 employer establishments and 168,178 non-employer or indeterminate establishments in professional, scientific, and technical services in 2025. Nationally, 73.8% of employer establishments were micro-businesses. This is a large, fragmented market whose smallest firms often have custom client workflows but limited operations capacity.[^6]

### Core workflow

Lead → proposal → client → engagement/project → tasks and approvals → time/cost → deliverable → invoice → renewal or follow-up.

### Competitive context

General work-management competitors are inexpensive but configuration-heavy. Airtable starts around US$20 per user per month annually, monday.com around US$9, ClickUp around US$7, Asana around US$10.99, and Teamwork around US$10.99–13.99 depending on the current plan structure. Purpose-built agency products add resource planning, budgeting, invoicing, and profitability: Productive has been listed from US$11 per user, while Teamwork’s more capable plans have been listed around US$25.99 per user and above.[^7][^8][^9]

Scheza’s opportunity is not another project board. It is **zero-configuration client operations**:

> **Describe how the firm wins and delivers work. Scheza builds the CRM, projects, approvals, utilization records, deliverables, and invoicing workflow together.**

### Recommended MVP

- Leads, companies, contacts, proposals, and engagements.
- Projects, milestones, deliverables, dependencies, and approvals.
- Lightweight time and expense tracking.
- Client request and approval portal.
- Invoice readiness and profitability snapshot.
- Recurring engagement templates.
- Automated reminders for missing client assets, approvals, and overdue invoices.

## Scheza Community

### Target segments

- Community centres and settlement organizations.
- Youth, employment, housing, food-security, and social-service programs.
- Professional and trade associations.
- Arts and cultural nonprofits.
- Small foundations and grant-funded initiatives.
- Faith-based organizations with structured programs.

Ontario nonprofits face rising demand and constrained capacity. In a 2025 Ontario Nonprofit Network survey, 85% reported increased demand, 22% said they could not meet demand, and 47% of organizations with paid staff reported vacancies. Nearly half of respondents operated with budgets below C$1 million. This creates a strong need for efficient administration but also high price sensitivity.[^10]

### Core workflow

Referral or application → eligibility/intake → participant or member → program enrolment → service delivery → attendance or case notes → outcome → funder report → follow-up.

### Product opportunity

Nonprofits often need a relational case/program system, but their processes vary by funder and program. This is an unusually good fit for generative architecture because the software can construct each organization’s program, participant, service, outcome, and reporting relationships without requiring a database consultant.

> **Scheza Community turns program descriptions and funder requirements into an operating system for intake, services, outcomes, and reporting.**

### Recommended MVP

- Participant, household, member, donor, volunteer, and partner records.
- Program and service-delivery tracking.
- Referral and eligibility workflows.
- Attendance, outputs, outcomes, and document collection.
- Volunteer scheduling.
- Grant deliverables and report-ready exports.
- Role-based access and sensitive-field masking.
- English/French public forms.

The initial niche should avoid high-risk clinical case notes. Start with membership, programming, attendance, referrals, volunteer coordination, and funder outputs. Pricing should be flat by organization or program rather than per seat, because nonprofits rely on part-time staff and volunteers.

## Scheza Care

### Best entry point

Licensed childcare centres, after-school programs, home-childcare agencies, and non-clinical community care providers. Do not enter by promising a general healthcare platform.

Ontario reported 5,989 licensed childcare centres and 532,525 licensed spaces in 2024–25, including a net annual increase of 153 centres. Nationally, 59.5% of child-care centres were operating at maximum capacity and 77.3% had an active waitlist in 2024. Child day-care services are overwhelmingly small organizations: 99.7% of establishments had 0–99 employees in 2025.[^11][^12][^13]

### Core workflow

Inquiry → waitlist → enrolment → child/family record → attendance → staff/room assignment → incident or observation → billing/subsidy record → family communication → compliance reporting.

### Competitive context

Canadian childcare products use varied pricing models. Published examples include CentreBloom from C$69 per centre per month, CRAFTS from C$50 per month, and SAEL from C$360 annually for home providers; several established platforms use custom quotes. Scheza therefore cannot rely only on being less expensive.[^14]

### Differentiation

> **Describe the centre, rooms, programs, staffing, forms, and reporting needs. Scheza builds the operating system around the way that centre actually runs.**

A strong product could combine waitlists, enrolment forms, attendance, staff tasks, incidents, inventory, inspection readiness, and management dashboards. However, childcare involves sensitive information about minors. Before launch, Scheza needs granular permissions, immutable audit history, document-retention controls, guardian consent records, stronger incident workflows, and a formal privacy/security review.

### Health boundary

Ontario’s PHIPA governs how health-information custodians collect, use, disclose, retain, transfer, and dispose of personal health information. PIPEDA also establishes accountability, consent, limiting collection, safeguards, access, and other principles for commercial handling of personal information. Scheza should therefore defer clinical records, diagnosis, medication, treatment, and electronic-health-record use until it has PHIPA-specific governance, detailed audit logs, breach procedures, interoperability analysis, and contractual controls.[^15][^16][^17]

## Scheza Distribution

### Target segments

- Food and beverage wholesalers.
- Building-material and industrial-parts distributors.
- Specialty importers.
- Local manufacturers with dealer or reseller networks.
- Equipment-rental and supply businesses.

Ontario had 19,746 wholesale employer establishments and 18,746 non-employer or indeterminate establishments in 2025. Nationally, 57.3% of wholesale employers were small businesses with 5–99 employees. Food merchant wholesalers alone included 2,232 employer establishments in Ontario.[^18][^19]

### Core workflow

Customer or dealer → quote/order → products → supplier purchase order → receiving → inventory allocation → delivery → invoice → returns or claims → reorder.

### Product position

> **Scheza Distribution connects customers, orders, products, suppliers, purchasing, and fulfilment in a system built around the distributor’s real workflow.**

The strongest wedge is exception management rather than a full ERP: backorders, low stock, supplier delays, special pricing, order status, return claims, and account follow-ups. This edition becomes attractive after Scheza has robust inventory transactions, units of measure, purchase orders, barcode support, accounting connections, and role permissions.

## Scheza Retail Ops

### Target segment

Independent retailers with one to ten locations, a moderate product catalogue, online and physical sales, and no dedicated operations analyst. Good micro-niches include specialty food, beauty supply, home goods, apparel boutiques, sporting goods, pet retail, and culturally specific grocers.

Retail is large and highly fragmented. Ontario had 50,223 retail employer establishments and 46,332 non-employer or indeterminate establishments in 2025; 97.7% of Canadian retail establishments had fewer than 100 employees.[^20][^21]

### What not to build

Do not start with a new POS, card processor, or ecommerce storefront. Square, Shopify, and Lightspeed already bundle transactions and inventory. Publicly reported pricing ranges from free entry plans to roughly US$89 per location monthly for more capable retail plans, with payment processing and hardware added separately. Competing head-on would force Scheza into payments, hardware, uptime, tax, receipt, returns, and omnichannel complexity before validating its advantage.[^22][^23]

### Better wedge

Scheza Retail Ops should sit above the POS:

POS/ecommerce data → generated operating model → purchasing, transfers, stock exceptions, supplier performance, tasks, incidents, promotions, customer segments, and management actions.

> **Connect the stores and describe the operation. Scheza builds the control room that shows what is selling, running low, delayed, overstocked, or losing margin.**

### Required connectors

- Square and Shopify first.
- QuickBooks or Xero.
- Lightspeed and Moneris later.
- CSV import as the fallback path.

The edition should initially be called **Retail Ops**, not simply Retail, to make clear that Scheza complements rather than replaces checkout software.

## Scheza Restaurant Ops

### Target segment

Independent full-service restaurants, cafes, bakeries, quick-service operators, and small groups with one to five locations. The ideal buyer already has a POS but still runs invoices, prep lists, supplier follow-ups, maintenance, incidents, and cost checks in paper, chat, and spreadsheets.

Ontario had 29,885 employer establishments in food services and drinking places in 2025; 77.8% were small employers with 5–99 employees. The pain is severe: 44% of surveyed Canadian restaurants were operating at a loss or breaking even in late 2025, while labour and food costs concerned 89% and 88% of operators respectively. High upfront costs and uncertain return on investment remain important barriers to adopting technology.[^24][^25][^26][^27]

### Competitive context

Restaurant software is fragmented by function. Toast starts with entry offers around US$0–69 per month but hardware, payment processing, and modules raise total cost; 7shifts offers scheduling from a free tier into paid per-location plans; MarginEdge publishes a price of US$350 per location per month for invoice, inventory, recipe, and cost management.[^28][^29][^30]

### Product position

> **Scheza Restaurant Ops turns POS and supplier data into daily actions—what to order, what costs changed, what task was missed, and what needs the owner’s attention.**

### Recommended MVP

- Invoice capture and supplier-price history.
- Products, ingredients, vendors, recipes, and basic counts.
- Opening, closing, prep, cleaning, temperature, and compliance checklists.
- Equipment and maintenance records.
- Incidents and corrective actions.
- Staff tasks, not payroll or full scheduling.
- Daily manager report and cross-location exception dashboard.

Scheza should integrate with POS and accounting systems rather than process transactions. Its differentiator is generating a restaurant-specific back-office workflow quickly and unifying operational exceptions—not recreating Toast, 7shifts, or MarginEdge feature for feature.

## Scheza Campus Ops

### Correct segmentation

“University” is not one customer problem. A university contains many operational units with different budgets, systems, risks, and buyers. The viable entry is **departmental operations**, not replacement of the student information system.

Promising micro-niches include:

- Faculty research administration.
- Departmental procurement and approvals.
- Student-service request and case triage.
- Co-op placement and employer relationship tracking.
- Continuing-education enrolment operations.
- Lab equipment and safety workflows.
- Events, room requests, and campus service operations.
- Advancement projects and alumni engagement for smaller units.

Educational services comprised 51,062 establishments in 2025, with 93% having 0–99 employees; the category includes schools, colleges, universities, and training centres. The number is not a count of universities, but it demonstrates a substantial broader education market that includes smaller, more accessible training organizations.[^31][^32]

### Competitive context

Salesforce Education Cloud targets recruitment, admissions, academic operations, student success, advancement, and financial workflows. Canadian nonprofit list pricing has been published at US$87 per user per month for Enterprise, US$145 for Unlimited, and US$375 for top agentic editions, under annual contracts. This confirms both strong demand and a large implementation/cost gap below enterprise platforms.[^33]

Higher-education workflows often span email, spreadsheets, disconnected portals, and rigid ERP or SIS products. Low-code workflow layers are marketed for admissions, procurement, research administration, finance, HR, and service requests without replacing the underlying SIS, LMS, or ERP.[^34]

### Product position

> **Scheza Campus Ops turns a department’s forms, approvals, cases, deadlines, and reports into a working system—without waiting for a custom IT project.**

### Entry strategy

- Begin with continuing-education providers, private colleges, research institutes, and individual university departments.
- Sell one workflow or department at a time.
- Position Scheza as an approved workflow layer, not an unofficial student database.
- Use enterprise controls: SSO, granular roles, audit logs, data retention, data-location commitments, accessibility conformance, API governance, service-level terms, and administrator oversight.
- Build integrations to Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, major SIS/ERP systems, and identity providers only when pilots prove repeated demand.

A university-wide product is a later-stage enterprise motion. The shorter path is a department with a visible backlog and a sponsor who can fund a controlled pilot.

## Scheza Workplace

### Correct segmentation

“Corporate business” is also too broad. Scheza should sell to a specific operations team with a neglected workflow, not to “the corporation.” Strong entry points include:

- Vendor onboarding and renewal tracking.
- Procurement requests and approvals.
- Facilities requests and asset tracking.
- Marketing intake and campaign operations.
- Employee onboarding and equipment assignment.
- Compliance evidence and policy acknowledgements.
- Customer implementation and handoff tracking.
- Franchise or branch operations.

### Competitive context

Horizontal workflow products already compete aggressively on price. monday.com starts near US$9 per seat monthly on annual billing, Airtable around US$20, and several alternatives between US$5 and US$25. Scheza cannot win enterprise teams merely by offering tables and automation.[^8][^35][^36]

### Product position

> **Describe the process that is stuck in spreadsheets and email. Scheza builds the governed intake, records, approvals, dashboard, and follow-up system around it.**

The differentiation is rapid generation plus governance: a complete working workflow, connected data model, sample environment, explanations, safe change management, and an operator that follows up on stalled work. Enterprise readiness—SSO, audit logs, environment controls, data retention, permission policies, integration governance, and procurement documentation—must precede a serious corporate push.

## Additional verticals

### Scheza Recreation

Gyms, studios, sports clubs, camps, arts programs, and event operators manage members, registrations, waivers, instructors, equipment, schedules, incidents, and renewals. Ontario had 6,650 employer establishments in arts, entertainment, and recreation in 2025; nationally, 96.2% of establishments had fewer than 100 employees.[^37][^38]

A narrow initial edition could target youth sports and class-based programs. Avoid payment-heavy booking competition initially; focus on enrolment operations, waivers, attendance, staff tasks, incidents, equipment, and program reporting.

### Scheza Rental

Equipment, event-rental, and specialty-rental firms manage assets, availability, reservations, inspections, damage, maintenance, pickup/return, deposits, and invoices. Ontario had 1,933 employer establishments and 3,678 non-employer or indeterminate establishments in rental and leasing services in 2025.[^39]

This edition has strong relational fit and can share inventory, work-order, scheduling, and invoicing infrastructure with Service and Property. It is a credible Phase 2 niche once asset availability and transaction histories are robust.

### Scheza Association

Trade groups, chambers, alumni associations, and professional memberships need organizations, members, committees, renewals, events, certifications, sponsorships, communications, and reporting. This can share most capabilities with Scheza Community but use commercially oriented language and membership billing.

### Scheza Franchise Ops

Small franchise systems and multi-location service brands need location onboarding, SOP attestations, inspections, incidents, action plans, local marketing requests, vendor records, and performance reporting. This has a smaller prospect pool but higher annual contract value and a clear generated-template advantage.

## Shared platform architecture

Scheza should be a **single multi-tenant platform with vertical packs**, not separate products or codebases. Each edition is a configuration, ontology, workflow library, integration bundle, and marketing surface on the same core.

### Core platform

Every edition should share:

- Natural-language business and workflow discovery.
- Generated relational data model.
- Contextual sample data before sign-up.
- CSV/Excel import with explainable mapping.
- Tables, cards, forms, dashboards, filters, and reports.
- Public and authenticated intake forms.
- Relationships, related records, activity history, and documents.
- Roles, permissions, sensitive-field controls, and Canadian hosting.
- English/French structure.
- Notifications, approvals, recurring reminders, and workflow execution.
- Learned organizational language and patterns.
- Governed digital-crew actions with approval thresholds.

### Vertical packs

Each pack should contain:

- Industry vocabulary and prompt questions.
- Canonical relational model.
- Workflow recipes and suggested statuses.
- Dashboard and KPI definitions.
- Sample records localized to the customer’s region.
- Industry forms, checklists, and reports.
- Compliance rules and retention policies.
- Integration adapters.
- Agent skills and action safeguards.

## Brand architecture

### Recommended model

Use an **endorsed suite under one master brand**:

- Scheza Service
- Scheza Property
- Scheza Professional
- Scheza Community
- Scheza Care
- Scheza Distribution
- Scheza Retail Ops
- Scheza Restaurant Ops
- Scheza Campus Ops
- Scheza Workplace

Do not create independent brands such as “ContractorOS,” “RetailOS,” and “CampusFlow.” Separate names dilute recognition, create multiple acquisition problems, and imply multiple products. The shared Scheza name communicates one platform that learns different kinds of operations.

### Naming rules

- Name the operating context, not every industry.
- Use a vertical only when workflows, language, or integrations materially differ.
- Avoid ambiguous labels: “Commerce” can mean ecommerce, retail, wholesale, or payments.
- Use “Ops” when Scheza complements an established transaction or system-of-record platform.
- Preserve Scheza as the product users log into; editions control onboarding, templates, navigation, integrations, and marketing.

### Proposed suite message

> **One generative operating system. Built differently for every kind of organization.**

Supporting line:

> **Scheza Service, Property, Professional, Community, Retail Ops, Restaurant Ops, Campus Ops, and Workplace share one intelligent platform that builds itself around the way each organization works.**

## Competitive strategy

Scheza competes with three different groups as it expands.

### Horizontal tools

Airtable, monday.com, ClickUp, Smartsheet, Notion, and Zoho sell flexibility. Their strength is breadth and mature collaboration. Their weakness for Scheza’s target buyer is setup: users must decide what to build, translate real operations into tables or boards, connect tools, and maintain the configuration. Current entry prices commonly range from about US$5 to US$24 per user monthly, with more advanced tiers higher.[^40][^7][^8]

Scheza must not market itself as “easier Airtable.” The stronger message is:

> **Airtable gives the organization building blocks. Scheza delivers the operating system already assembled around that organization.**

### Vertical incumbents

Vertical systems know their industries deeply and provide specialized workflows, integrations, and compliance. Their weaknesses vary: rigid setup, expensive add-ons, contracts, transaction lock-in, or an inability to model unusual processes.

Scheza should not claim deeper functionality on day one. It should win customers whose work falls between spreadsheets and heavyweight vertical software, then integrate with incumbents when they are the trusted system of record.

### Enterprise platforms

Salesforce and enterprise low-code systems sell extensibility, governance, and broad ecosystems. Education Cloud pricing illustrates the gap: even nonprofit list tiers range from US$87 to US$375 per user monthly before implementation considerations. Scheza can serve departments and midsize organizations that need custom structure without an implementation program—but only after developing credible governance and security capabilities.[^33]

## Pricing architecture

A single global pricing model will not fit every segment. However, Scheza should retain its principle of predictable pricing and avoid charging for every viewer, volunteer, field worker, or occasional participant.

### Recommended models

| Edition family | Preferred metric | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Service / Professional | Business size or active workflow volume | Predictable for small teams; aligns with operational scale |
| Property / Rental | Unit or asset bands | Familiar and value-linked |
| Community / Association | Organization plus program bands | Avoids penalizing volunteers and participants |
| Care | Location plus enrolment bands | Matches centre economics and support burden |
| Retail / Restaurant | Per location, with connector tiers | Aligns with existing category norms |
| Distribution | Order or location bands | Reflects data and workflow intensity |
| Campus / Workplace | Department or workspace contract | Fits procurement and governance requirements |

### Pricing guardrails

- Include unlimited or generous internal collaborators in SMB tiers.
- Keep public submitters, customers, tenants, students, participants, and volunteers free.
- Charge more for integrations, governed automation, higher storage, service commitments, and enterprise controls.
- Do not impose unpredictable AI-credit pricing on the core workflow.
- Validate willingness to pay separately in each vertical; a restaurant location, nonprofit program, agency, and university department have different budgets and buying processes.

## Go-to-market sequence

### Stage 1: Prove the engine

Keep the current Service beachhead and reach repeatable activation and retention. The PRD already defines the proper gate: import activation, sustained week-four usage, and growth in records under management. Do not expand because adjacent markets look large; expand when the core generation, import, workflow, and retention loops work consistently.[^1]

### Stage 2: Adjacent packs

Build Property and Professional packs using existing primitives. Run founder-led design partnerships with five to ten organizations in each niche and require real spreadsheet imports. Measure:

- Time from prompt to a usable system.
- Percentage of generated schemas retained without major redesign.
- Import completion.
- Number of old tools displaced.
- Weekly active collaborators.
- Repeated workflow events.
- Four- and eight-week retention.
- Support hours per tenant.

Release only the pack whose users show stronger retention and lower implementation effort.

### Stage 3: Mission-driven and regulated operations

Launch Community after granular roles, sensitive fields, audit history, retention controls, and outcome reporting mature. Move into Care only after privacy review and compliance-specific requirements have been implemented.

### Stage 4: Connected operations

Launch Retail Ops and Restaurant Ops after reliable POS/accounting connectors and an event-normalization layer exist. The market is compelling, but the product must produce trusted daily actions from external transaction data.

### Stage 5: Institutional pilots

Pursue Campus Ops and Workplace through partners, departmental pilots, and paid discovery. Avoid a broad enterprise sales team until Scheza has:

- SSO and directory provisioning.
- Granular role and field permissions.
- Immutable audit logs.
- Data-retention and legal-hold controls.
- Accessibility documentation.
- Security review materials and incident procedures.
- Integration governance.
- Backup, recovery, uptime, and support commitments.

## Vertical launch scorecard

Before approving a new edition, require evidence against this scorecard:

| Test | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| Pain | 15+ interviews show the same high-frequency operational failure |
| Data fit | 80% of target workflows map to existing core objects and relationships |
| Generation quality | 90% of pilot schemas need only minor edits after generation |
| Migration | 60% of claimed pilots import real data within seven days |
| Habit | 40% use the product in four consecutive weeks |
| Multi-user value | 50% invite at least one colleague |
| Economic value | Buyer can name a saved cost, reduced risk, faster cycle, or recovered revenue |
| Willingness to pay | At least five unrelated organizations accept the target price |
| Support load | Onboarding can be completed without custom development |
| Compliance | Legal/privacy requirements are documented and technically enforceable |
| Distribution | At least two scalable acquisition channels are identified |
| Operator potential | Three or more recurring actions can progress from suggest to approve to run |

## Product roadmap changes

The current architecture is well suited to expansion, but the suite vision requires several platform capabilities earlier than a trades-only roadmap would.

### Near-term core

- Industry-pack registry and versioning.
- Better relationship types and reusable object definitions.
- Configurable workflow/status engines.
- Calculated fields and aggregate metrics.
- Files, photos, document categories, and retention policies.
- Recurring tasks, schedules, and reminders.
- Record ownership, assignment queues, and service-level timers.
- Fine-grained table, field, record, and action permissions.
- Immutable activity and change history.
- Branded external portals beyond public forms.
- Approval chains and e-signature connections.

### Integration layer

- OAuth connector framework.
- Webhooks and scheduled imports.
- Canonical models for contacts, products, invoices, payments, locations, employees, and transactions.
- Conflict handling and sync logs.
- Accounting connectors first.
- POS/ecommerce connectors second.
- Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, SSO, and enterprise systems third.

### Governed operator

The future digital crew remains coherent across every vertical:

- Service: bookkeeper, dispatcher, sales follow-up.
- Property: maintenance coordinator, renewal assistant, owner-reporting assistant.
- Professional: project coordinator, billing assistant, client-success assistant.
- Community: intake coordinator, program administrator, reporting assistant.
- Restaurant: purchasing analyst, compliance coordinator, daily-ops assistant.
- Retail: replenishment analyst, supplier follow-up assistant, store-ops coordinator.
- Campus: case-routing assistant, approvals coordinator, deadline monitor.

Every action type should begin as a suggestion, progress through explicit approval, and earn limited autonomy only after repeated clean approvals. Customer-facing, money-moving, safety-sensitive, student, child, tenant, and health-related actions should retain higher thresholds and strong auditability.

## What to avoid

### Avoid horizontal dilution

“Scheza for any business” weakens the message and returns the product to blank-canvas competition. Every landing page should speak to a specific buyer, operational problem, and workflow even though the underlying platform is shared.

### Avoid simultaneous launches

Launching Service, Retail, Restaurant, Campus, and Workplace together would multiply onboarding, integrations, support, compliance, SEO, and sales motions. One additional pack should be validated at a time.

### Avoid core-system replacement

Do not initially replace:

- POS and payment processing.
- General ledgers and payroll.
- Student information and learning-management systems.
- Electronic health records.
- Property trust accounting.
- Enterprise resource planning.

Win the fragmented operational layer first, then deepen selectively.

### Avoid compliance theatre

Canadian hosting is valuable but does not by itself make a product compliant. PIPEDA imposes broader principles around accountability, purpose, consent, limiting collection, safeguards, access, and challenges. Ontario health deployments add PHIPA-specific duties and potential interoperability and audit requirements. Each regulated pack needs its own legal, privacy, security, and retention review.[^16][^17][^41][^15]

### Avoid generic AI claims

“AI-powered” does not explain value. Each edition should promise an operational result: fewer unbilled jobs, faster maintenance resolution, fewer missed client approvals, report-ready program data, visible food-cost changes, or shorter departmental approval cycles.

## Final portfolio recommendation

The suite should be presented as:

> **Scheza is the generative operations platform that builds, improves, and helps run the systems small and midsize organizations depend on every day.**

The recommended portfolio is:

1. **Scheza Service** as the commercial beachhead.
2. **Scheza Property** and **Scheza Professional** as the first adjacent experiments.
3. **Scheza Community** as the mission-driven expansion.
4. **Scheza Care** after compliance hardening.
5. **Scheza Distribution**, **Retail Ops**, and **Restaurant Ops** after the connector platform matures.
6. **Scheza Campus Ops** and **Scheza Workplace** as later departmental enterprise products.

The central strategic idea is not to sell the same empty workspace to every sector. It is to maintain one intelligent platform and let each buyer encounter a product that already speaks their language, understands their records, proposes their workflows, imports their history, and becomes more useful as it learns how that particular organization operates.
